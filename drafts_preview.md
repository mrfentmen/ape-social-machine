## LINKEDIN DRAFTS (as the Ape AI company page)

### li1 (the research gap story)

Retail investors don't have an information problem anymore.

They have a translation problem.

Everything you need is technically public: the 10K, the earnings call, the guidance revision, the 13F. It's all out there. And it's all written in a dialect designed to be skimmable only by people who already know the vocabulary.

Most smart people, engineers, nurses, small business owners, open an earnings release, scroll for 40 seconds, and close the tab. Not because they don't care. Because the effort of deciphering it feels worse than buying whatever someone on TV mentioned.

That gap is why Ape AI (AskApe.com) was built.

It's an AI research companion for retail investors. You ask questions in plain English, like "why did this stock move today" or "what changed in this quarter's guidance" or "what does this company actually do," and you get sourced, structured answers instead of a wall of SEC formatting.

Not a broker. Not auto trading. Not signals. A research tool that treats you like an adult.

The market doesn't reward people with the most information anymore. It rewards people who can process what they already have.

What's the first thing you'd ask if research took minutes instead of evenings?

Educational purposes only. Not financial advice.

### li2 (earnings season discipline)

Earnings season is where retail investors find out whether they have a process or just a portfolio.

A stock drops 8%. The headline tells you the number. It never tells you the sentence that matters, which is usually buried three paragraphs deep in the release: the guidance change, the margin note, the one time charge doing the heavy lifting.

If you can't distinguish "the business broke" from "the quarter missed a noisy estimate," you're not investing. You're reacting, with extra steps.

This is the boring, unglamorous work that actually compounds: read the release, find the number that moved the stock, decide if it changes the thesis. Most people skip it because it's tedious, not because it's hard.

We built Ape AI (AskApe.com) to do that tedious layer. It pulls the actual figures and explains what changed and why the market cares, so the decision stays yours but the archaeology isn't.

No predictions. No guaranteed anything. Just fewer people selling good companies over a headline.

How do you currently separate signal from noise during earnings week? Genuinely curious what people's process looks like.

Educational content only. Not financial advice.

### li3 (portfolio visibility / honesty)

A quick exercise: name every position you hold, and one sentence on why you own it.

Most people get to position four or five and start hedging.

Not because they're careless, but because nobody taught them that a portfolio is a set of claims about the world. "I own this because I think margins stay strong" is a claim. Claims can be checked. Positions without claims are just lottery tickets with extra paperwork.

That's the gap between owning stocks and having a strategy, and it's bridgeable with boring tools: a holdings list, a concentration check, a regular "what changed" review.

Ape AI (AskApe.com) automates the boring part. Connect a brokerage and it maps your holdings, surfaces concentration, and tells you what actually changed this week, in plain English.

The thinking part stays with you. It should. That's the whole point.

If you actually did the exercise: how many positions could you defend in one sentence?

Educational purposes only. Not financial advice.

### li4 (tools over gurus)

Unpopular opinion in fintech marketing: most people don't need more confidence. They need better friction.

The standard pitch is the opposite, all "trade with conviction" and "find your edge," because confidence sells subscriptions. But look at what actually hurts retail returns: overtrading, reacting to headlines, position sizes based on vibes.

The fix isn't more conviction. It's more structure between impulse and order.

That structure looks like: check what actually changed, compare it to your own thesis, look at your portfolio's exposure, then decide. Same info the pros use, minus the $20k/seat terminal.

Ape AI (AskApe.com) is that middle layer, an AI research companion that answers "what happened and does it matter" in plain English, for people who'd rather verify than vibe.

We're not selling conviction. We're selling the pause button.

Educational purposes only. Not financial advice.

### li5 (why Ape AI exists)

Ape AI exists because of a gap the industry keeps ignoring.

Two groups consistently talk past each other.

Group one: finance professionals, with terminals, models, and vocabulary that took a CFA to acquire. Group two: everyone else, with a brokerage app, a YouTube habit, and genuine questions the industry has zero interest in answering at their level.

The advice aimed at group two is either condescending ("just index") or reckless ("these 5 stocks NOW"). Almost none of it treats them as adults who could do real research if the research were legible.

Ape AI is that legibility layer. AskApe.com: ask a question about a stock, an earnings report, or your own portfolio in plain English, get a structured, sourced answer. Brokerage connections let it see what you actually hold so the context is real, not generic.

Not advice. Not automation. A translator between you and the paperwork.
